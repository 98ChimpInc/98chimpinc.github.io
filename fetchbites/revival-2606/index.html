The Honest History

What actually happened in 2025

February 2025 ... Meta says no We applied for official access to Instagram messaging. Rejected and decommissioned, no reason given. We later learned Meta was tearing out and replacing its entire permission system that exact month ... apps in the queue were rejected in bulk, by machines. Bad timing, not a bad idea.
Spring–Summer 2025 ... the heroic workaround We built a robot that controlled a real phone and tapped through Instagram like a human, with AI reading screenshots. It worked ... but it was slow, costly, fragile, and impossible to scale.
August 2025 ... smart pause We shelved it rather than burn money. The recipe-card designer and the AI recipe brain ... the genuinely hard parts ... were finished and excellent.
June 2026 ... the rules changed Three shifts in our favour. That's this brief.

The Strategy, In One Picture

Two doors into one kitchen

In 2025 we bet everything on a single door that Meta controlled. The new plan has two doors. If Meta ever closes theirs again, we lose a marketing channel ... not the company.

Both doors feed the same kitchen. Door 1 needs Meta's blessing to scale ... Door 2 never asks permission, and it works for TikTok too (which has no door 1 at all).

The Question Everyone Asks

"Can't Meta block the Share button too?"

No ... and it's worth understanding why. There is no software trick (and no SDK) that lets one app read another app's messages; phones isolate every app in its own locked room. That's why our 2025 robot was the only workaround, and why it hurt. The Share button is different: it isn't us reaching into Instagram ... it's the phone's operating system handing the post out to us, at the user's request.

Our 2025 robot tried to pick the lock on the left ... slow, fragile, expensive. The Share button uses the handshake on the right ... instant, free, and dependable.
