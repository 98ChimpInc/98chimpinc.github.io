In a no-convolution MLP setting, a neuron split into independent sub-units beats a parameter-matched MLP… but only once there's enough data, and mostly not for the reason we expected.

Project Boreal tests Sardi et al. (2017)'s 'neuron as multiple independent threshold units' as an ANN architecture: K input partitions, K independent thresholds, one hard max. Across ~3,000 seeded runs on MNIST and CIFAR-10, the advantage appears at moderate-to-large training-set sizes, the best K rises 4 → 8 → 16 as N goes 2,000 → 5,000 → 10,000, and only 12.1% of the best effect is cleanly spatial. Baselines are deliberately weak (no convolution); all claims are relative within that class. The original low-data hypothesis failed… and is reported first.

  • CIFAR-10, K=16, N=10,000: +0.0491 vs matched MLP
  • Best K: 4 → 8 → 16 at N = 2,000 / 5,000 / 10,000
  • 30 seeds · Holm-corrected · pre-registered
  • ▼ Founding hypothesis: falsified (reported below)

01

The idea: Model I vs Model III

A conventional neuron sums all its inputs and applies one threshold. A Model III neuron¹ first splits its inputs into K disjoint groups. Each group has its own independent threshold. The neuron then outputs the hard max over the groups… the strongest group wins, and groups never pool evidence before thresholding. The extra cost is only (K-1) extra biases per neuron (+0.045% of parameters at CIFAR K=2; +0.675% at K=16), so the comparison against a parameter-matched MLP isolates the neuron model itself.

  1. K disjoint groups of inputs.
  2. An independent threshold per group.
  3. Hard max: no cross-group summation before thresholding.
What this is not. This is functional, not biophysical, realism: no Hodgkin-Huxley dynamics, morphology, or synaptic physiology. The alternative Model II comparison (the model Sardi et al.'s experiment was designed to exclude) has not been run; we name it here as the clearest gap.

¹ Based on Sardi et al. (2017), Sci Rep 7, 18036: a neuron behaving as multiple independent threshold units. The connecting insight (Sardi et al. 2017 → ANN architecture) dates to Shahin Zangenehpour's April 2018 article "Can Old Neurons Teach Us New Tricks?".

04

Methods & provenance

Protocol throughout: 30 seeds, Holm-Bonferroni correction, paired t-tests governing ordering claims, pre-registration committed before results, bit-level determinism spot-checks before results were merged, and gates that blocked work when criteria failed, including one gate that stopped a planned sweep on real data and was not amended after the fact. Corrections are recorded in place (a retracted crop verdict, a corrected audio-tau reading, a winner's-curse correction on β selection). Five falsified predictions are logged as falsifications, not reframed. A manifest of the project record was externally timestamped (RFC 3161, DigiCert TSA, Oct 2 2026); that anchor is 'no later than' evidence only.

  • ~3,000 seeded runs
  • 30 seeds · Holm-Bonferroni
  • Determinism: 0.000000 / 0.00000000
  • ▼ 1 gate blocked a sweep

Interpretability, narrowly: "which sub-unit fired" is a discrete, auditable fact. Nothing stronger is claimed.

05

Limits & open controls

  1. Mechanism mostly unexplained: 65.6% of the best advantage is non-spatial. Without a parameter-matched maxout control (designed but not run), 'Model III' may substantially be 'hard selection over a partition helps MLPs', closer to known maxout/mixture territory.
  2. Missing controls (named, not designed here): Model II and a parameter-matched maxout control have not been run; nor have CIFAR N=20,000, a batch-size control at K≥32 (K = batch size 32; ≥50% structural dead-row floor at K=64), a dead-row census beyond one (N, seed) cell, a cue-amplitude sweep, or a patch-size sweep.
  3. Baselines are weak by design: CIFAR-10 with no convolution (MLP tops at 0.4315) and flattened MNIST. All claims are relative within that controlled class only; not evidence against modern architectures.
  4. Temporal results are strictly 'at β=0.95': in Exp 4, the temporal treatment and task difficulty are the same parameter.
  5. CREMA-D margins are an open confound, not a result (unmasked readout over zero-padding; normalisation over the padded tensor). See Space × time.
06

Space × time and real data

Unified 2×2 (K∈{1,4} × β∈{0,0.95}): the spatial × temporal interaction is real and Holm-significant at every N ≥ 4000, peaking at N=8000 (Unified early_bit 0.9129 at N=8000). But persistence costs count accuracy, growing with N: the unified model is a trade, not a strict improvement. Chart C.

▼ NEGATIVE FEASIBILITY RESULT · TEXT ONLY

CREMA-D (real audiovisual emotion, Phase 3a): Gate B passed decisively (audiovisual 0.5191 vs best unimodal 0.4646, +0.0545 integration headroom). Gate A failed on video (+0.0141 at β=0.95 / +0.0146 at β=0.7165 vs +0.03 required; 95% CI [+0.0017, +0.0275]; video probe 0.4230; audio margin +0.0945; chance 0.1667; duration alone predicts emotion at 0.2312). The 2×2 sweep was blocked and the gate was not amended. Confounds: 25.9% of timesteps are zero-padding under an unmasked readout, and normalisation was computed over the padded tensor, so these margins are an open confound, not a result. This is a negative feasibility result.
